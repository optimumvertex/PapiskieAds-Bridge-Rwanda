Where new arrivals overpay

Three places the foreigner tax hits hardest

Locals in Rwanda are warm and welcoming — but in any setting without a fixed price tag, sounding like a newcomer is expensive.

01 — Guciririkanya

Open-air markets

There are no price tags at Kimironko or the local stalls. Ask "how much" in English and the opening price often doubles on the spot.

02 — Moto-taxis

Every ride is a negotiation

Fares are agreed before the ride starts. Without the local phrase for "you're overcharging," a 500 RWF trip quietly becomes 2,000.

03 — Renting a home

Verbal deals, real risk

Expats are often quoted inflated USD rents with no written agreement — leaving them with zero protection if terms change later.

A preview of the guide

Four phrases that change the price

The full PDF covers markets, moto-taxis, and renting. Here's the one word that does the most work.

Say thisKinyarwandaWhy it works
"How much is this?" Ni bangahe? Point at the item as you say it — signals you're not a first-timer.
"Reduce the price!" Gabanya! The single most useful word in the whole guide. Say it with a smile.
"You're overcharging!" Urahenda For moto-taxis and services — not goods.
"Tenancy agreement" Amasezerano y'Ubukode Never rent on a verbal promise — ask for this by name.
